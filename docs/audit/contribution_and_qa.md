# Contribution, supervisor Q&A and presentation — Tasks 6–8

## Three-sentence contribution paragraph

Our project provides auditable Python interpretations of UGO-SMOTE, BFCH-SMOTE, EC-EBO and SSHR, with explicit source ambiguities and implementation departures rather than a claim of exact author-code equivalence. We compare those implementations with NORES and SMOTE on Ecoli4 and Yeast4 using a common observed-label budget, fixed repeated group folds, training-only preprocessing and paired unlabelled-versus-empty-pool controls. We document metric tradeoffs, resampling shortfalls and retrospective pseudo-label correctness, finding lower mean F1 with U in all four conditions while retaining the Yeast4/GNB ranking and G-mean improvements; this is an implementation/evaluation contribution, not a new oversampling method.

Evidence, sentence by sentence: [audit_protocol.md, fidelity table](audit_protocol.md); `scripts/run_sshr_small_study.py:31–38,78–143` and `src/sshr_evaluation.py:397,411–415,482–505`; [recomputed_ablation.csv](recomputed_ablation.csv), [pseudo_label_audit.csv](pseudo_label_audit.csv), both original `diagnostics.csv` files rows 7/13, and [claims_table.md](claims_table.md).

## Limitations paragraph

This is a descriptive pilot on two datasets and two classifiers under a simulated approximately 50% training-label budget, not a naturally unlabelled-data study or the papers' complete benchmark protocols. Repeated folds share observations, so sample SDs are descriptive; no between-method significance tests or generality claims follow, and tuned-threshold NORES/class-weighting controls were not included. Author-code equivalence remains unverified, several implementations intentionally resolve conflicting paper rules, and SSHR uses a printed objective that can exceed the paper's stated F1 bound; consequently its outcomes describe this audited interpretation. The study measures global metrics and training-only distance proxies, not true generated labels, known-distribution overlap, held-out minority-subregion improvement or isolated causes of the U effect.

Evidence: `scripts/run_sshr_small_study.py:31–38,96–143`; `src/sshr_evaluation.py:324–340,430–480,498–505`; `src/sshr_clustering.py:72–89`; [audit_protocol.md](audit_protocol.md); [audit_numbers.md](audit_numbers.md). The UGO PDF version-identity gap is recorded separately in [audit_protocol.md, provenance gap](audit_protocol.md).

## One proposed next step — HYPOTHESIS, NOT IMPLEMENTED, NOT A RESULT

**Hypothesis:** a predeclared uncertainty-gated admission rule for SSHR could improve the quality of selected U rows and reduce downstream false-positive harm relative to the current Eq.7 ranking. The motivation is observed selected-row correctness of 170/313 and 96/273; those figures do not establish that the rule will work. Evidence for the motivation: both original `diagnostics.csv` files rows 7/13 and [pseudo_label_audit.csv](pseudo_label_audit.csv).

**Proposed test design, not an existing feature:** fit/cross-fit an auxiliary classifier using observed L only, and admit an Eq.7 candidate only when it has sufficiently strong minority support and sufficiently low defined uncertainty. Fix the rule/thresholds through prespecified settings or inner observed-label validation; do not use hidden U gold labels or outer-test outcomes for admission or tuning. Compare against ungated SSHR, the empty-U control and appropriate operating-point controls, recording rejected candidates, selected counts, fallback volume and shortages. Keep the with-U clustering design fixed between gate/no-gate arms where isolating admission is the aim; otherwise label the comparison as a joint effect. These are proposed requirements, not claims that a gate/module or experiment already exists. Existing separation and fallback policies motivating the proposal: `src/sshr.py:103–118`, `src/sshr_evaluation.py:430–437`, `docs/SSHR_METHOD_DECISIONS.md:241–244`.

**Falsification criterion:** reject the practical hypothesis if a prespecified independent evaluation shows no useful precision/FPR improvement, worsens F1/recall, or only looks better because it rejects almost every candidate or silently changes sample volume. High confidence can still be wrong; a gate is not a membership oracle. The study's known precision/geometry shortcomings motivate this caution, not a guarantee. Evidence: [audit_numbers.md, pseudo-label table](audit_numbers.md), `src/sshr_evaluation.py:324–340`.

This is a post-hoc hypothesis motivated by already-inspected outcomes; confirmatory testing needs a new, genuinely untouched evaluation, not repeated tuning against these same public test results. Resolve or explicitly predeclare the SSHR objective interpretation before attributing gate effects. A full prior-art comparison, including SLEO, is required before any novelty claim; an ordinary confidence/uncertainty filter is not automatically a new method. SLEO is absent from the six-method pilot, so performance/novelty relative to it is **UNVERIFIED**. Evidence: `scripts/run_sshr_small_study.py:33`; `src/sshr_clustering.py:72–89`; [paper source and page references: sleo](paper_sources.md#sleo).
